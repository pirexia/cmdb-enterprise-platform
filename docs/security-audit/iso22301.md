# ISO 22301:2019 Business Continuity Management Audit — CMDB Enterprise Platform v2.0.1

**Audit date:** 2026-04-17
**Standard:** ISO 22301:2019 (Business Continuity Management Systems — BCMS)
**Scope:** CMDB Enterprise Platform v2.0.1
**Auditor:** Compliance Review — DevSecOps

---

## Executive Summary

The CMDB Enterprise Platform provides core recovery mechanics — automated database backup (`scripts/db-backup.sh`), Docker `restart: unless-stopped` policies, git-tagged rollback points, and a structured `update.sh` with automatic health-check-driven rollback — that place it at a foundational operational level for business continuity. Based on documented procedures, the achievable Recovery Point Objective (RPO) is approximately 24 hours (daily backup at 02:00 AM) and the Recovery Time Objective (RTO) is estimated at 30–90 minutes, assuming the host is available and a practised operator follows the manual restore procedure documented in the sysadmin manual. The most critical ISO 22301 gap is the absence of a documented, tested Business Continuity Plan (BCP) and Business Impact Analysis (BIA) — backup scripts and restart policies exist, but there is no formal continuity strategy, defined RTO/RPO targets, or evidence of DR procedure testing. Additionally, backups are stored on the same host as the running platform with no off-site replication, meaning a single host failure could destroy both the production environment and all recovery data simultaneously.

### Clause 4 — Organisational Context and CMDB Criticality

#### Clause 4.1 / 4.2 — Understanding the Organisation and Interested Parties

#### Current State

The CMDB Enterprise Platform manages configuration items (CIs), contracts, licenses, documents, and vulnerabilities for an organisation's IT infrastructure. The CI schema captures `criticality` (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / MISSION_CRITICAL), `environment` (DEVELOPMENT / TESTING / STAGING / PRODUCTION), `businessImpact` (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL), `recoveryPriority` (1–5), `spofRisk`, `containsPii`, and `dataClassification` fields — indicating that asset-level continuity metadata is structurally supported. The platform itself runs on Docker Compose behind nginx on a single host (RHEL 9 or compatible).

#### Gap

There is no formal document establishing the criticality of the CMDB platform to the organisation's business operations — for example: what business processes depend on access to CMDB data, which teams are impacted if the platform is unavailable, and what the financial or operational impact of unavailability is. ISO 22301 Clause 4 requires this context to be documented as the foundation for the BIA and continuity strategy. Without it, recovery priorities cannot be formally justified.

#### Estimated Impact of CMDB Unavailability

Based on the platform's scope (IT asset inventory, vulnerability tracking, contract and license management), the following business impacts are reasonably inferred:

- IT operations teams cannot locate asset ownership or technical contacts during incidents.
- Vulnerability remediation workflows are blocked (CVE triage and assignment requires CMDB data).
- Contract and license renewals cannot be managed, risking unplanned service interruptions.
- Audit and compliance teams cannot access immutable audit logs for regulatory reporting.
- Integrations with CrowdStrike and Greenbone cannot import vulnerability data.

#### Recommendation

1. Produce a one-page Organisational Context statement for the CMDB platform, listing dependent business processes, impacted teams, and the criticality designation (suggest: HIGH).
2. Identify and document interested parties: IT operations, security operations, compliance/audit, procurement, senior management, and regulatory bodies.

### Clause 6 — Business Impact Analysis and Business Continuity Objectives

#### Current State

The CI data model natively captures per-asset RTO and RPO values (integer fields in `CICreateSchema`: `rto` and `rpo`), enabling asset-level recovery planning within the platform's managed inventory. However, these are data fields for the assets the CMDB manages — not formal continuity objectives for the CMDB platform itself.

#### Gap

No Business Impact Analysis (BIA) has been conducted for the CMDB platform as a system. Specifically:

- No formal RTO or RPO targets are documented for the platform itself.
- No Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption (MTPD) is defined.
- No Minimum Business Continuity Objective (MBCO) is specified (e.g., "read-only access to the last backup is acceptable during recovery").

**Estimated practical RTO/RPO based on documented procedures:**

| Metric | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| RPO | ~24 hours | Daily cron backup at 02:00 AM; data written after last backup is lost |
| RTO | 30–90 minutes | Documented manual restore: git checkout rollback tag + Docker rebuild + `gunzip` restore pipeline + health check |
| MTPD | Undocumented | Not formally defined; suggested 48 hours based on operational criticality |

The 30–90 minute RTO estimate assumes: the host is available, a practised operator is on-call, the backup file is accessible on the local filesystem, and no schema migration conflicts arise. If the host must be reprovisioned from scratch, RTO would extend to 2–4 hours minimum.

#### Recommendation

1. Conduct a BIA workshop with stakeholders, producing a document that covers: business processes dependent on the CMDB, impact of unavailability over time (1h, 4h, 24h, 72h), financial and operational impact estimates, and formal RTO/RPO/MTPD targets.
2. Publish these targets in a BCMS Policy document approved by management.
3. Validate that the existing backup and recovery procedures can meet the agreed targets through a timed DR test.

### Clause 8.3 — Business Continuity Strategy

#### Current State

The following continuity strategy elements are implemented:

**Database backups:** `scripts/db-backup.sh` performs `pg_dump | gzip -9` daily (cron at 02:00 AM per sysadmin manual), storing compressed archives in `BACKUP_DIR` (default `/opt/cmdb/backups`). Retention is 30 days by default (configurable via `RETENTION_DAYS`). The script uses `set -euo pipefail`, checks container availability before running, and logs results with timestamps. Log rotation via `logrotate` is documented for `/var/log/cmdb-backup.log`.

**Container restart policy:** All production services use `restart: unless-stopped`. The PostgreSQL container has a health check (`pg_isready`, 10-second interval, 5 retries) and the backend container depends on PostgreSQL health before starting.

**Update rollback:** `update.sh` creates `rollback/<timestamp>` git tags, performs mandatory pre-update backup, and auto-rolls back if the post-update health check fails within 120 seconds.

**Document storage:** The sysadmin manual acknowledges that `DOCUMENTS_STORAGE_PATH` must be included in the backup strategy alongside PostgreSQL.

#### Gap

- **Single location backup:** Backup archives are stored on the same host as the running platform (default `/opt/cmdb/backups`). A disk failure, ransomware attack, or host destruction would simultaneously eliminate the live database and all backups. ISO 22301 Clause 8.3 requires the continuity strategy to address multiple failure scenarios, including loss of the primary site.
- **Document storage not backed up:** `db-backup.sh` covers only the PostgreSQL database. No companion script or documented procedure backs up the binary document store (`DOCUMENTS_STORAGE_PATH`). Documents attached to contracts, licenses, and CIs would be permanently lost in a host failure.
- **No redundancy or high-availability configuration:** The platform runs as a single Docker Compose stack on one host. There is no active-passive failover, database replication, or load-balanced redundancy.
- **No off-site replication:** The sysadmin manual's NFS configuration for document storage could serve as a foundation for off-site storage, but this is documented as an optional configuration rather than a required BCM control.

#### Risk Level

**High** — Co-located backups and absent document-store backup are the most critical technical BCM gaps.

#### Recommendation

1. Implement off-site backup replication for database archives: add an `rclone` or `aws s3 cp` step at the end of `db-backup.sh` to upload each new archive to an S3-compatible object store or remote SFTP target.
2. Create `scripts/docs-backup.sh` to archive `DOCUMENTS_STORAGE_PATH` via `tar -czf` with the same naming convention and retention logic as `db-backup.sh`, scheduled at the same time.
3. Add archive integrity verification to `db-backup.sh`: `gunzip -t "${BACKUP_FILE}" && echo "${LOG_PREFIX} Integrity OK"` — fail and alert if the test fails.
4. Document the off-site backup locations and access credentials in the DR runbook (stored separately from the primary host).
5. Evaluate deploying a hot-standby PostgreSQL replica using streaming replication to reduce RPO to near-zero for database data.

### Clause 8.4 — Business Continuity Plans

#### Current State

The sysadmin manual (`docs/SYSADMIN_MANUAL.md`) contains detailed operational procedures that function as a partial BCP:

- Section 6.3 documents the database restore procedure (`gunzip | psql`).
- Section 8.1 documents `update.sh` with automatic rollback on failure.
- Section 8.2 documents manual rollback using `git checkout rollback/<timestamp>` + Docker rebuild.
- Section 8.3 documents database restore after rollback when DDL migrations must be reverted.
- Section 4.3 documents TLS certificate renewal.
- Section 9 (Troubleshooting) provides symptom-based diagnostic procedures for common failure modes.

#### Gap

- The sysadmin manual is a general operations guide, not a Business Continuity Plan. A BCP is a formally approved, role-specific document activated during a declared continuity event. Key elements absent from the current documentation:
  - A declaration of a continuity event (who decides the CMDB is "down" vs. "degraded"?).
  - Contact tree: who is called, in what order, with what escalation timeline.
  - Roles and responsibilities during a continuity event (Incident Commander, Recovery Lead, Communication Lead).
  - Communication templates for internal stakeholders and, if applicable, regulatory notifications.
  - A minimum viable service definition: what is the minimum the platform must provide to be declared "recovered"?
  - Recovery sequence order: PostgreSQL first, then backend, then frontend, then nginx — currently implicit in `docker compose up` dependency ordering but not explicitly stated as a recovery sequence.
- The DR runbook does not cover the scenario where the **entire host is lost** (e.g., VM destruction, hardware failure): no procedure exists for provisioning a replacement host from scratch, restoring Docker volumes, and re-applying the `.env` configuration from a separately-stored copy.

#### Recommendation

1. Produce a standalone Business Continuity Plan document (separate from the sysadmin manual) covering: scope, activation criteria, roles, contact tree, communication plan, recovery sequence, and minimum viable service definition.
2. Add a "Host Loss Recovery" section to the DR runbook, covering: OS/Docker installation, git clone of the platform, volume restoration, `.env` restoration from a securely stored copy, and end-to-end smoke test.
3. Store a copy of the BCP, DR runbook, and emergency credentials in a location physically separate from the production host (e.g., a secure document management system or sealed physical envelope with a named custodian).

### Clause 8.5 — Recovery Procedures

#### Current State

The sysadmin manual documents the following recovery procedures:

- `gunzip -c backup_<timestamp>.sql.gz | docker exec -i cmdb-postgres-prod psql -U cmdb_admin -d cmdb_db` (database restore from backup).
- `docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml build --no-cache && docker compose up -d` (container rebuild and restart).
- `git checkout rollback/<timestamp>` (code version rollback).
- `docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml restart backend` (certificate hot-reload after TLS renewal).
- A post-restore verification query: `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM configuration_items`.

The `update.sh` script automates the most common recovery scenario (failed update) with a 120-second health-check-driven automatic rollback.

#### Gap

- **No test evidence.** ISO 22301 Clause 8.5 requires that continuity plans be exercised and that the results be documented. There is no record of a recovery drill, tabletop exercise, or timed failover test.
- The post-restore verification check (`COUNT(*) FROM configuration_items`) is minimal. It confirms the table is accessible but does not verify data integrity, index health, or application-layer functionality.
- No procedure exists for partial recovery scenarios: for example, recovering only from a specific transaction-level backup if corruption is detected mid-day (point-in-time recovery), or recovering the document store independently of the database.
- The auto-rollback in `update.sh` uses a 120-second health check window which only verifies the `/health` HTTP endpoint — it does not verify that SMTP alerts, MFA, SSO, or LDAP authentication are functional after recovery.

#### Recommendation

1. Schedule and conduct a DR test at least annually: restore from backup to a test host and document the measured RTO. Record the test result in a DR test log.
2. Extend the post-recovery verification procedure to include: a test login (including MFA), a test API call to `GET /api/cis`, a document download, and a test email send via the alert engine.
3. Enable PostgreSQL Write-Ahead Log (WAL) archiving and configure point-in-time recovery (PITR) to reduce RPO from 24 hours to near-continuous for database data.
4. Extend the `update.sh` health check to validate additional application functions beyond the basic `/health` endpoint.

### Clause 9.1 — Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis, and Evaluation

#### Current State

The following monitoring mechanisms are in place:

- `GET /health` endpoint returning `{"status":"ok","timestamp":"..."}` — used by `update.sh` and documentable for external monitoring systems.
- PostgreSQL container health check: `pg_isready` with 10-second interval, 5 retries, 15-second start period.
- `restart: unless-stopped` on all containers provides automatic recovery from process crashes.
- Sysadmin manual monitoring table defining alert thresholds: disk > 80%, response time > 2s, memory > 1.5 GB, error rate > 10/min, certificate expiry < 30 days.
- `scripts/check-docs-storage.sh`: shell script to alert at 70% and 85% disk usage on `DOCUMENTS_STORAGE_PATH` via email.
- Backup script logs with timestamps to `/var/log/cmdb-backup.log`.
- Daily email alert engine scanning for EoL, contract expiry, and critical vulnerabilities.
- Three scheduled cron jobs within the application: daily alert (08:30), audit log purge (03:00), trusted device cleanup (02:00).

#### Gap

- The `/health` endpoint is a liveness check only — it confirms the Express process responds but does not verify database connectivity, disk space, or dependent service availability.
- No structured log aggregation: application logs go to Docker stdout/stderr only, with no integration to a SIEM, ELK stack, or equivalent. Logs are not centrally stored and are lost if the container is destroyed.
- No metrics for continuity-relevant signals: backup success rate over time, mean recovery test result, service uptime percentage.
- The sysadmin monitoring table defines thresholds but there is no automated alerting for most of them (disk space on backups, API response time, memory usage) — these require manual inspection or integration with a separate monitoring tool.
- Audit log purge at 365 days (default) means evidence for long-running investigations or regulatory audits may be destroyed. ISO 22301 and NIS2 both suggest longer retention for security-relevant logs.

#### Recommendation

1. Enhance `/health` to include: `db: ok/fail` (simple `SELECT 1` query), `disk_free_gb` for both backup and document storage paths, and `last_backup_age_hours` by checking the mtime of the most recent backup file.
2. Integrate Docker logs with a log aggregation system (ELK, Loki/Grafana, or Splunk) to enable persistent, searchable log history that survives container lifecycle events.
3. Expose application-level continuity metrics via `GET /api/admin/continuity-status` (admin-only): last successful backup timestamp, backup file count, last alert scan result, audit log record count.
4. Increase `AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS` to a minimum of 730 days (2 years) in production deployments.

### Clause 10 — Improvement, Non-Conformity, and Post-Incident Learning

#### Current State

The `SECURITY_AUDIT.md` documents a structured remediation history with named issues (#8 through #34), version-tagged fixes, and control references (ISO 27001 Annex A). This demonstrates a feedback loop from identified vulnerabilities to implemented fixes. The `update.sh` script includes downgrade protection and the update log at `logs/update_<timestamp>.log` provides an audit trail for deployment events.

#### Gap

- No formal post-incident review (post-mortem) process or template exists. When an incident occurs (e.g., a failed deployment, a backup failure, an authentication anomaly), there is no documented procedure for conducting a root-cause analysis, recording lessons learned, and tracking corrective actions.
- No continual improvement register or BCMS improvement log is maintained.
- The `SECURITY_AUDIT.md` is the closest analogue to a non-conformity register but it covers security findings only, not operational continuity incidents.
- No BCMS internal audit or management review procedure is documented, as required by ISO 22301 Clauses 9.2 and 9.3.

#### Risk Level

**Medium** — Without a post-incident learning process, the same continuity failures may recur without systemic improvement.

#### Recommendation

1. Create a post-incident review template and procedure: trigger criteria, review meeting format, root-cause analysis method (5-Whys or Ishikawa), corrective action tracking, and owner/deadline assignment.
2. Maintain a BCMS improvement log (could be a simple table appended to a `BCMS_LOG.md` or tracked in an issue tracker) recording: incident date, description, root cause, corrective action taken, and verification date.
3. Schedule an annual BCMS internal audit against ISO 22301 clauses, with results reported to management.
4. Conduct a post-exercise review after each annual DR test, updating the BCP and DR runbook based on findings.
